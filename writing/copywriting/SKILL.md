# Draft copy for the actual offer

Use the supplied audience, product facts, channel, goal and voice. Inspect existing copy or source material when relevant. Ask about a missing fact when it would change the promise or make a claim unreliable; otherwise produce a useful draft with clearly identified assumptions.

Lead with the reader's problem or desired outcome and a specific credible benefit. Make the offer and next action clear. Match structure and length to the channel: a landing page, email, headline and button need different treatments.

Support measurable claims, testimonials, comparisons and guarantees with supplied or verified evidence. Do not invent customers, results, urgency, scarcity or product capabilities. Use a placeholder or a qualified factual statement when evidence is missing.

Preserve the established brand unless a change is requested. Offer alternatives when they help a decision, not a mandatory set for every sentence. Deliver the requested copy in a usable form, with brief rationale only where helpful. A separate brief approval is not required before drafting; publishing or sending follows the user's authorization.
